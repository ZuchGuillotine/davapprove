01 · Read the system correctly

The useful unit of approval is a specific scope of work, on a specific design revision, released for a specific milestone. A project can be ready for structural review while blocked from clearing; it can have a building permit and still lack authority to install a septic system or work in water.

This report maps ordinary residential development, additions/ADUs, subdivisions and common commercial site work in Kitsap County. It adds conditional state, federal, tribal and utility branches. It is a research-backed workflow specification, not a parcel-specific approval or a complete rule engine for every industrial, federal or tribal project. Sources checked November 5, 2026. Local numerical thresholds, vesting and individual permit conditions must be attached to the actual project.

Evidence labels: “Documented” means the cited authority describes the requirement. “Model” means a proposed Davapprove coordination rule. “Confirm” means the responsible agency must resolve applicability or procedure. The diagrams are model views of documented relationships; they do not assert that every displayed branch applies to every parcel.

Edges have different meanings. A report can be needed to start review; a decision can be needed to issue another permit; an issued permit can remain ineffective until a waiting period ends. These must not be represented by one undifferentiated “depends on” edge.

03 · Resolve the septic–civil–wetland–shoreline–designer loop

The coordination gate below is a recommended product workflow, not a new government approval. It is the main opportunity to prevent avoidable redesign.

Shared variableAffected disciplinesWhat to settle before freezing a layout
Wetland edge, rating, stream location and applicable buffersBiologist, surveyor, septic designer, civil, architect, plannerField evidence, reviewer acceptance and where survey is needed. County wetland code explicitly carries wetland/buffer geometry into septic, utility, grading and other plans. [04]
Primary and reserve septic areas; bedroom/flow capacitySeptic, designer, civil, landscape, ownerReserve area is an occupied design constraint, not spare land for stormwater or future additions. Resolve existing-system clearance versus BSA route. [12] [13]
Grades, groundwater and infiltrationGeotechnical, septic, civil, structuralDifferent soil investigations answer different questions. Reuse observations where appropriate, but do not treat a septic test pit as automatic stormwater infiltration or foundation certification.
House footprint, height, floors and retaining wallsDesigner, structural, civil, shoreline, septicCompare footprint alternatives against all constraints before detailed structural work. Smaller footprint can increase height or retaining needs.
Driveway, turning area, hydrants and fire flowCivil, fire reviewer, water utility, biologistPotable availability is not proof of fire flow. A longer drive increases impervious area and may create wetland crossings. [08] [10]
Outfall and off-site drainage routeCivil, geotechnical, adjacent owner, WDFW, Ecology, CorpsConfirm lawful discharge rights and whether work in water activates permits; infiltration infeasibility may create a wholly new aquatic branch. [30] [27] [35]
Construction staging and temporary disturbanceAll site disciplines and contractorExcavation access, stockpiles, erosion controls and utility trenches must fit too. Do not show only permanent improvements.
  1. Build the common base: obtain records, identify contradictions and survey uncertainty, flag critical features.
  2. Run feasibility studies in parallel: septic soils/reserve, ecology, geotechnical/drainage, water and access. Share proposed investigation locations before disturbing sensitive ground.
  3. Compare feasible layouts: keep alternative house, wastewater and discharge strategies explicit. Identify potentially fatal constraints before polishing architecture.
  4. Resolve interfaces: every discipline accepts the same geometry revision and a list of remaining assumptions. Ask agencies focused questions that affect layout.
  5. Release detailed design: proceed only after high-cost uncertainties are resolved or explicitly accepted by the owner.
  6. Submit coordinated packages: applications may run in parallel where allowed. Each package identifies the exact common revision.

Model the envelope as scenario-dependent feasible geometry, not merely parcel minus buffers. Access, wastewater, drainage, constructability and service rights must all fit. Buffer reductions and variances are alternate unapproved scenarios until granted; they must not enlarge the approved envelope in advance.

05 · External approvals are overlapping, conditional gates

JARPA standardizes an application, not an approval. It can support multiple aquatic permits, but every authority retains its decision and may require separate submissions and supporting information. [36]

BranchLogic and workflow consequence
Local critical areas + shorelineBoth can constrain the site. Shoreline substantial-development exemption does not eliminate substantive shoreline requirements or a needed CUP/variance. Local approval of a shoreline CUP/variance also needs Ecology approval. [25]

RFI workflow: capture the comment verbatim with source page, affected node, agency, requested evidence and response owner. Classify it as missing information, code conflict, discretion, inter-plan conflict or new scope. Prepare one revision manifest, identify every changed variable, obtain discipline signoff and submit to each affected agency. If agencies conflict, request a documented joint resolution; do not quietly satisfy one by violating another.

Appeals: create an appeal child node with forum, filing deadline, standing, record, scope, stay status and outcome. Shoreline, local land-use, health, federal and other state decisions do not share one appeal process. The operative notice and governing law determine deadlines. A pending appeal can be a legal hold, a financial risk, or both; represent them separately.

08 · Required professional, recommended professional, or owner work?

An owner can prepare many applications and some building plans. That does not create a general right to self-certify regulated engineering, surveying or septic design.

Can an owner design a septic system?

For the ordinary Kitsap path, use a licensed OSS designer or qualified professional engineer. KPHD Ordinance 2025-01 §6.K requires licensed design. State WAC 246-272A-0230 allows a local health officer, at discretion, to let a resident owner design a single-family system outside 200 feet of a marine shoreline; that is not an automatic owner entitlement. No routine Kitsap owner-design pathway was established by the reviewed local materials. Treat any claimed exception as requiring a specific written health-officer determination before relying on it. [11] [15]

Owner installation is a different question. KPHD §12.B provides a limited resident-owner installation route with system, location, competency and designer-oversight conditions. It does not waive licensed design. [11]

Can an owner do structural calculations?

Owner-prepared prescriptive residential plans can be possible; owner calculations do not substitute for a required professional seal. RCW 18.08.410 exempts specified residential design—including up to four dwelling units—from architect licensure. It is an architectural exemption, not blanket authorization to practice engineering. RCW 18.43.130 contains no general homeowner engineering exemption. Select the actual code path and obtain the building official’s requirements for any engineered portions. [16] [17] [18]

The practical distinction is selecting code-permitted members and details within all prescriptive limits versus performing a project-specific engineered design. A building can combine both. Bremerton’s linked engineering handout explains that distinction, but it is dated 2007: its numerical criteria and submission instructions must not be used as current code. [20]

How to handle an agency rejection

A failed technical review should create a bounded revision task, not simply restart the project. Example: the health reviewer finds the reserve field conflicts with the proposed stormwater feature. Freeze both conflicting features; have septic and civil compare compliant alternatives; check those alternatives against wetland geometry and building/access needs; obtain owner choice; update the common revision; revalidate only the affected assumptions internally. Submit wherever each agency requires—Bremerton’s published process explicitly describes all-department review after revisions. The software can target coordination effort even where agency resubmittal rules are broader. [42]

A substantive denial is different: record the rejected proposal and basis, then branch to redesign, an authorized exception/variance, appeal or abandonment. Never convert denial into a “pending task” that silently lets construction proceed.

11 · Implementation specification for Davapprove

The overall system is a directed graph with typed edges, milestone predicates and revision history. At one frozen revision the execution dependencies should usually be acyclic; design feedback creates cycles across successive revisions. Find strongly connected design components and manage them as coordinated work packages. A topological sort alone cannot decide applicability, property rights or professional judgment.

Rules that prevent false green lights

  1. Use three-valued applicability: true, false and unknown. Unknown cannot satisfy a construction release.
  2. Distinguish “review can start,” “decision can issue,” “work can start” and “occupancy can occur.”
  3. Evaluate AND gates over required branches; evaluate OR gates only over legally available alternatives.
  4. Do not equate consultant completion, agency receipt, agency acceptance and permit effectiveness.
  5. Propagate change only through declared dependent fields, but expand review when the dependency is uncertain.
  6. Keep all approvals attached to their approved revision; do not overwrite the past with the current plan.
  7. Show the smallest unresolved prerequisite sets blocking the chosen milestone, with a named next action and responsible party.
  8. Maintain agency-specific review clocks and a separate owner-consultant clock. An agency’s public turnaround is not a deadline guarantee.
  9. Attach authoritative text and source version to each rule. Preserve interpretation and reviewer confirmation separately.
  10. Make the final work-release decision auditable and human-confirmed until validated against real permit files.

Useful measurements

Track inter-discipline conflicts found before submittal; revision rounds caused by mismatched plans; days waiting on owner, consultant and agency separately; time from parcel intake to a defensible coordinated envelope; number of formerly accepted decisions requiring amendment; and unresolved assumptions at detailed-design release. Measure these on real projects before claiming cost or approval-time savings.
